NORTHWIND LOGISTICS, INC.
THIRD QUARTER FISCAL 2024 EARNINGS CONFERENCE CALL
OCTOBER 29, 2024

CORPORATE PARTICIPANTS

Dana Whitfield - Chief Executive Officer
Marcus Ohl - Chief Financial Officer
Priya Raghunathan - Vice President, Investor Relations

OPERATOR

Good morning and welcome to the Northwind Logistics third quarter fiscal 2024
earnings conference call. All participants will be in listen-only mode. After
today's presentation there will be an opportunity to ask questions. Please note
this event is being recorded. I would now like to turn the conference over to
Priya Raghunathan, Vice President of Investor Relations. Please go ahead.

PRIYA RAGHUNATHAN

Thank you and good morning everyone. Before we begin, I would like to remind you
that today's call contains forward-looking statements subject to risks and
uncertainties. Actual results may differ materially. Please refer to our filings
with the Securities and Exchange Commission for a discussion of these risks. We
will also refer to non-GAAP measures; reconciliations are available in our
earnings release. With that I will turn the call over to Dana.

DANA WHITFIELD

Thanks Priya, and good morning. The third quarter was our strongest of the year.
Revenue in the third quarter was 1,071.4, up 8.1% year over year. Cold Chain
continued to lead, growing 17.9% in the quarter, and we opened seven new
refrigerated facilities.

I want to spend a moment on the Cold Chain investment cycle because it is the
single biggest driver of where this business goes over the next three years. We
have now committed approximately 640 of capital to that segment since the start
of fiscal 2023, and we are seeing returns come through ahead of our underwriting.
Segment operating margin in Cold Chain reached 14.9% in the third quarter,
compared with 12.1% a year ago.

On the Ground Freight side, the market remains soft. Volumes were roughly flat
and pricing was up low single digits. We are managing that business for cash
rather than for growth, and I think that is the right posture until the freight
cycle turns.

Let me hand it to Marcus for the financial detail.

MARCUS OHL

Thank you Dana. Third quarter revenue was 1,071.4 compared with 991.1 in the
prior year period. Operating income was 118.9 compared with 94.3, and operating
margin was 11.1% compared with 9.5%.

Adjusted EBITDA in the quarter was 187.6, up 16.4% year over year. Net income
was 74.2 and diluted earnings per share were 0.95, compared with 0.71 in the
prior year quarter.

Cash provided by operating activities in the quarter was 164.3. Capital
expenditures were 88.7, giving free cash flow of 75.6 for the quarter.

We ended the quarter with cash of 476.9 and total debt of 1,861.0, putting net
leverage at 2.7 times trailing twelve month Adjusted EBITDA.

Turning to guidance. We are raising our full year fiscal 2024 revenue outlook to
a range of 4,150 to 4,200, from the prior range of 4,080 to 4,160. We are also
raising full year Adjusted EBITDA guidance to a range of 695 to 710, from 675 to
700. We now expect full year capital expenditures of approximately 315.

QUESTION AND ANSWER SESSION

OPERATOR

We will now begin the question and answer session. Our first question comes from
Elena Vasquez.

ELENA VASQUEZ

Good morning. Dana, on Cold Chain, can you talk about how much more capacity you
intend to add and what that does to the capital intensity of the business?

DANA WHITFIELD

Sure. We have 22 facilities coming online across fiscal 2024, and our current
plan calls for roughly 15 to 18 in fiscal 2025. So the pace moderates. Capital
intensity peaks this year at around 7.5% of revenue and we expect it to trend
back toward 6% over the following two years as the build-out normalizes.

ELENA VASQUEZ

And the returns on those?

DANA WHITFIELD

We underwrite to a mid-teens unlevered return. The cohort we opened in fiscal
2023 is tracking above that. I would not extrapolate the whole program from one
cohort, but it is encouraging.

OPERATOR

Our next question comes from Tomas Berger.

TOMAS BERGER

Thanks. Marcus, on the balance sheet — you took leverage down to 2.7 times. Where
do you want that to sit, and does the buyback compete with deleveraging?

MARCUS OHL

Our stated target is 2.5 times or below through the cycle. We are close. On the
buyback, we repurchased 4.1 million shares fiscal year to date for 214.0. We do
not view it as competing with deleveraging at current levels — free cash flow
covers both. If the freight environment deteriorated materially we would
prioritize the balance sheet.

TOMAS BERGER

Understood. And the fuel hedge position?

MARCUS OHL

We are hedged on approximately 60% of anticipated diesel consumption on a rolling
twelve month basis. That is up from roughly 50% a year ago, which reflects our
view that fuel volatility is more likely to hurt than help from here.

OPERATOR

Our next question comes from Rachel Ndiaye.

RACHEL NDIAYE

On Cross-Border, growth has been the slowest of the three segments for several
quarters now. Is that a structural issue or a cyclical one?

DANA WHITFIELD

Mostly cyclical, with some structural mix. Cross-Border revenue grew 3.3% for the
year and that is below where we would want it. The customs clearance
modernization we have been investing in should take a day out of average transit
time by the middle of fiscal 2025, and we think that is worth a couple of points
of growth.

OPERATOR

This concludes our question and answer session. I would like to turn the
conference back over to Dana Whitfield for closing remarks.

DANA WHITFIELD

Thank you all for joining. We look forward to updating you on our fourth quarter
and full year results in February.

OPERATOR

The conference has now concluded. Thank you for attending today's presentation.
